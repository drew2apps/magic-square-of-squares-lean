# Request for an audit

For a reader who knows elliptic curves over ℚ. The Lean part of this repository is checked by the Lean kernel and needs no audit beyond reading the statements. The part described here is computed, was written with Claude (an AI model made by Anthropic), and has been checked by nobody else. I am not a number theorist. Each question below can be answered separately; an answer to one of them is useful. This file is a working checklist for the repository, not part of a paper.

**Two kinds of answer.** For every number below there are two different questions: did the program return this value (anyone can rerun it), and does the algorithm behind it prove the bound unconditionally. The second is what I cannot judge. Please say which one an answer is about.

## What is claimed

For coprime `1 ≤ n < m ≤ 1000` there are 425 ratios `m : n` for which congruences do not exclude a 3×3 magic square of squares with `(b + d) : (h + f) = m : n`. A solution is a rational `t` at which five quadratic forms in `t` are squares (`G1_FULL_SYSTEM.md`, "The system"). 268 ratios are closed by a quotient elliptic curve of rank 0. 122 more are closed by the Demjanenko–Manin argument on a quotient elliptic curve that occurs at least twice in the Jacobian and has rank exactly 1. Two more, (820,799) and (889,320), are counted since 23 November 2026 in a third tier, on an empty fake 2-Selmer set returned by Magma's `TwoCoverDescent` for a curve of genus 2 (`GAUSS_DESCENT.md`, section 4; transcripts in `scripts/gauss_descent/magma_twocover_raw.txt`); what would be audited there is the construction of the two curves and whether the public calculator's answer can be relied on, and one of the two ratios rests on a single finished curve. Since 25 November 2026, 15 more are counted (and three of the four-point tier below are counted with them), closed by the same argument on the two elliptic quotients of `y² = F0·F8·GL` (`G1_FULL_SYSTEM.md`, Result 4; the bound and the listing rechecked in exact arithmetic by `scripts/g1_full/sigma_exact.py`). 9 ratios are open. This request is about those 122, about the 18 closed since 25 November 2026 on the curves of `F0·F8·GL` (the questions to check there: the case `|a| = |a′|`, the height step of the Möbius map, which side of the height bound is used where, and the index bound from the lower bound for `ĥ`; `G1_FULL_SYSTEM.md`, Result 4), and about the 9 closed by a version of the argument with four points, for rank 2 and 3 (closure 4 below; 6 of them are counted in that tier now). The 9 are a separate, weaker tier: the principle is the same classical method with four maps instead of two, but the identities, the constants and the lattice step were derived here and have been read by nobody else. It has its own document, `FOUR_POINTS.md`, with proofs of the elementary steps and eight questions. `scripts/g1_full/dm_evidence.txt` has one line for each, and `dm_log.jsonl` the raw output of the scripts.

## Software

Python 3.13.18; passagemath 10.8.14 (Sage), installed with `pip install passagemath-schemes passagemath-eclib passagemath-symbolics cypari2`; PARI 2.17.4 through cypari2; eclib as shipped with passagemath-eclib; Magma 2.29-10 through the public calculator at magma.maths.usyd.edu.au/calc. Commands are run in `scripts/g1_full/`. Each script prints one JSON record per pair; the records below are the ones that were printed, copied from `dm_log.jsonl` without its bookkeeping fields (`of`, `run`, `method`, `sec`).

## Four closures, worked out

Heights are Sage's (twice Silverman's `ĥ`). `B` is Sage's `silverman_height_bound()`. `S = m² + n²`.

**1. Curve `E04`, pair (15,8).** Command: `python3 dm.py - 15,8`.

```
{"pair": [15, 8], "rank": [1, 1], "gens": "mwrank", "G": "-83521", "hG": 3.1576167237813184, "B": 29.241874456487658, "amax": 27, "ntors": 4, "ncand": 111, "t": {"1": "degenerate", "-1": "degenerate"}, "nt": 57, "status": "closed"}
```

`κ = 2Sn² = 36992`, `E: y² = x(x + 130050)(x + 36992)`, in Sage `EllipticCurve([0, 167042, 0, 4810809600, 0])`. A solution `t` gives `P, P′ ∈ E(ℚ)` with `x(P) = κt²`, `x(P′) = κ/t²`, so `x(P)·x(P′) = κ²`. Rank 1 (PARI `ellrank` gives `[1, 1]`; eclib and Magma agree). Torsion of order 4. Generator `G` with `x(G) = −83521 = −289²` (eclib, `gens(proof=True)`), `ĥ(G) = 3.1576`, `B = 29.242`. Write `P = aG + T`, `P′ = a′G + T′`. From `|h(x(Q)) − ĥ(Q)| ≤ B` and `|h(x(P)) − h(x(P′))| ≤ 2 log κ`: `|a² − a′²|·ĥ(G) ≤ 2B + 2 log κ = 79.52`. So either `|a|, |a′| ≤ 25`, or `|a| = |a′|`, and then `P′ = ±P + T″` is an equation for `x(P)` for each torsion point `T″`. The script lists `|a| ≤ 27`, solves the equations, and gets 111 candidate abscissas. The only rational `t` at which the five forms are squares are `t = ±1`, which give degenerate squares.

**2. Curve `D1`, pair (631,71).** Command: `python3 dm2.py --upper=1 631,71` (the option supplies the upper rank bound 1 from Magma, because PARI gives `[1, 3]`).

```
{"pair": [631, 71], "curve": "D1", "rank": [1, 1], "upper": "external: --upper=1 (Magma, see magma_check.txt)", "gens": "pari point, saturated at primes <= 9 (height lower bound 0.1816)", "G": "0", "hG": 12.548181437530385, "B": 121.37383378653512, "amax": 21, "ntors": 2, "ncand": 43, "nt": 1, "t": {"0": "degenerate"}, "status": "closed"}
```

`E: Y² = x³ + c1x² + c0c2x + c0²c3` with `c3 = 4S²m²n²`, `c2 = −16Sm³n³`, `c1 = 4S²(m² − n²)²`, `c0 = −16Smn(m² − n²)²`. With `z = t + 1/t`, a solution gives `P, P′` with `x(P) = c0/z`, `x(P′) = −c0/z`, so `|a² − a′²|·ĥ(G) ≤ 2B`. `G` is the point with `x = 0`; `ĥ(G) = 12.5482`, `B = 121.374`, so `|a|, |a′| ≤ 19` or `|a| = |a′|`; the script lists `|a| ≤ 21`; torsion of order 2; 43 candidates; only the degenerate `t = 0`. That `G` generates: PARI `ellsaturation` at the primes up to 9; the index is at most 8 because `ĥ ≥ 0.1816` on non-torsion points (Sage, `height_function().min(0.01, 20)`). Rank: Magma's `TwoPowerIsogenyDescentRankBound` gives 1; the request, as sent, is

```
m:=631; n:=71; S:=m^2+n^2; D2:=(m^2-n^2)^2; c3:=4*S^2*m^2*n^2; c2:=-16*S*m^3*n^3; c1:=4*S^2*D2; c0:=-16*S*m*n*D2; E:=EllipticCurve([0,c1,0,c0*c2,c0^2*c3]); E:=MinimalModel(E); G,mp:=TorsionSubgroup(E); T:=mp((Order(G.1) div 2)*G.1); hi:=TwoPowerIsogenyDescentRankBound(E,T); print "BOUNDS", 0, hi;
```

and it is in `magma_raw.txt` with the response. This line, its partner (351,280) and the line for (724,665) (run on its partner (1389,59)) are the only three that rest on Magma alone; the last one is also closed by the method of closure 4, with PARI's bounds.

**3. Curve `E0L`, pair (423,332).** Command: `python3 dm3.py 423,332`.

```
{"pair": [423, 332], "curve": "E0L", "rank": [1, 1], "model": ["0", "-1", "0", "-10810266634575227101441", "180302397457159147910876363714305"], "ntors": 4, "gens": "pari point, saturated at primes <= 3 (height lower bound 6.3039)", "G": ["-27869819136", "21446103082364927"], "hG": 25.539739392571548, "B": 42.52991460522845, "C1": 94.27115544209109, "K": 180.02413183310793, "rho": 1, "W": 7.576630582558059, "amax": 11, "nalg": 0, "npts": 92, "worst_observed": 35.40671163564093, "t": {}, "status": "closed"}
```

`C: y² = F0(t)·GL(t)` with the rational point `t = m/n`; a solution `t` gives points of `C` at `t` and at `−t`. `t` is not an even function on the curve, so with `σ = 1/(t − m/n)` the script uses `|2h(σ(Q)) − ĥ(Q) − ĥ(R0 − Q)| ≤ K = C1 + log 2 + 2B`, where `R0` is the second point over `t = m/n` (derivation in `G1_FULL_SYSTEM.md`, "The bound for `E0L` in symbols"). `model` is the minimal model on which `G` is given. Here `R0 = G + torsion`, and the bound computed from these values is `|a| ≤ W + 1/2 = 8.08`; the script lists `|a| ≤ 11`, 92 points, none of which gives a square. `worst_observed` is the largest value of the left side on the listed points; it must be below `K`.

```

```

A solution `t0` gives four rational points `Q1, …, Q4` of `C: y² = F0·GL`, above `t0, −t0, 1/t0, −1/t0`. With `a_i = Q_i − R0/2`: (i) `N(a_i) = ĥ(2Q_i − R0)/4` is within `[−eL, eU]` of `H = h(t0)`; (ii) `|⟨a_1, a_2⟩|` and `|⟨a_2, a_4⟩|` are at most `κ`. Both come from identities of the form `x(…) = (…)²` on the model `Y² = x(x − α)(x − β)`, from elementary bounds for the heights of the resulting fractions, and from the bounds `−U ≤ h(x) − ĥ ≤ BUP` (`FOUR_POINTS.md`, Sections 2 to 4). In rank 2 the Gram determinant of `a_1, a_2, a_4` vanishes, so `z = a_1 ∓ a_4` has `ĥ(z) ≤ c*(H) = (e²/2 + 4κ²)/(H − eL)`, `e = eL + eU` (in Lean: `G1.fourPoint_rank2_bound`). With `H* = 748`: either `H < H*` and `Q1` is one of the 328 listed points, or `ĥ(z) ≤ 24.6`, and the enumeration finds no lattice vector other than 0 up to that height (the two basis vectors have heights 27.3 and 40.3), so `z` is one of the 4 torsion points, and `t(Q − z)·t(Q) = −1` is solved for each. No candidate passes the five-form test. The record also carries the certificate that the two basis points are saturated at 2, 3 and 5 (`sat`: the auxiliary primes; the index is at most `kmax = 6`), which `check_evidence.py` verifies without PARI or Sage, and the number of integer vectors in each list (`lists`), which it counts again.

## Questions

1. **Rank.** Are PARI's `ellrank` (2-descent with the Cassels pairing, run on the curve and on the isogenous curves) and Magma's `TwoPowerIsogenyDescentRankBound` unconditional upper bounds, as used here? For (631,71), (351,280) and (724,665), is Magma's answer alone enough, and how would you confirm it?
2. **Generators.** Is the value returned by Sage's `height_function().min(0.01, 20)` a proved lower bound for `ĥ` on all non-torsion points of `E(ℚ)`, so that saturation at the primes up to `⌊√(ĥ(P)/λ)⌋` proves that the saturated point generates? (Sage's documentation says that the value is established rigorously. For the four-point closures the saturation itself is now certified independently of PARI and checked in CI, `satcert.py`; for the 122 rank-1 lines it still rests on Sage and PARI.)
3. **Silverman's bound on both sides.** The software documents `B` as a bound for `h − ĥ`. `scripts/g1_full/height_sides.py` derives `ĥ − h ≤ B − h(j)/12 + 0.218` from two statements: at the finite places the naive local height is at least the canonical one, for any integral model (Cremona, Prickett and Siksek 2006, Proposition 8), and Silverman's bound at the real place (1990, p. 737, as quoted in their Theorem 15). It checks the inequality for all 122 models in exact arithmetic. Are the two statements used correctly in that script (normalisation of the heights, the real place of ℚ treated with the bound for complex points)? The first paper was read from its text with an AI model; Silverman's paper has not been seen, only the quotation in their Theorem 15.
4. **The comparison for `E0L`** in `dm3.py`: sum and product of the two abscissas as quadratics in `σ`, the constant `C1`, and the resulting bound on `|a|`.
5. **Heights of the generators.** The heights `ĥ(G)` used by the scripts are double-precision values from Sage. `scripts/g1_full/height_certify.py` replaces them by enclosures in exact rational arithmetic: `ĥ(G)` lies between `(h(x(2ᵏG)) − BUP)/4ᵏ` and `(h(x(2ᵏG)) + U)/4ᵏ`, where `2ᵏG` is computed exactly with the duplication formula (`k = 6` or `7`), `U` is as in question 3, and `BUP` bounds `h − ĥ` by the local bounds of Cremona, Prickett and Siksek (Proposition 8 and Table 1: `α_p` by Kodaira type and Tamagawa number, plus `ord_p(D/D_min)/6` for a non-minimal model) and Silverman's bound at the real place. With the lower ends of the enclosures the bound on `|a|` stays below the listed range in every line, by at least 9%. Is the use of Table 1 correct (in particular for a model that is integral and not minimal), and is the doubling enclosure the right way to certify a height?
6. **The singular fibres** (`G1_FULL_SYSTEM.md`, Result 1): the character-sum bound `32N ≥ 2p − 100 − 270√p` and the count of roots behind it.
7. **The density sketch** (Result 1b).
8. **The argument with four points** (closure 4; `FOUR_POINTS.md` has the argument and its own list of eight questions): the three identities are checked by computer algebra; the questions are the elementary height bounds (Lemmas A to C there; the divisibility statements for the common factors are in Lean, `G1FourPoints.lean`; the size estimates and their combination are not), whether the two lattice inequalities, which are in Lean, are the right statements for the Gram determinant in rank 2 and the block argument in rank 3, the completeness of the two listings and the argument that the exact enumeration contains them, and the saturation bound from Hermite's constant. For (740,539) the second independent point came from Magma's four-descent with class groups under GRH; the point is checked to lie on the curve, so GRH should not matter. Is that right?
9. **The last pair** (`LAST_PAIR.md`; an experiment, not in the census). A self-contained statement about two curves of genus 1: the only rational `t` with `d·Q₁(−it)` and `d·Q₂(−it)` both squares in `ℚ(i)` are `±1`, for (627,364) and `d = 521266`. Nine things to check, (a) to (i), each small: (a) Theorem 1.2, Proposition 3.2 and Theorem 2.2 of Checcoli, Veneziano and Viada (Forum Math. Sigma 7, 2019; arXiv:1602.04097) as quoted in section 5 there, with their normalisations of `ĥ`, `h₂` and `deg`; they were read by an automated reader, twice. (b) Lemma 2: the curve of solutions has components of genus 9, and no combination `uα + vβ` of the two maps to `E₁` is constant, by the action of `(ℤ/2)³` on four differentials. (c) Lemma 4: is the bound for `h₂(C)` from the points above roots of unity and Zhang's inequality used correctly? (d) Lemma 6: are the sets that `mw_sieve.py` computes modulo `p` all the possible reductions of the pair of points (the two primes above `p`, the signs, the twist, `t̄ = ∞`)? `planted_control.py` tests this on real points. (e) Since section 18 of `LAST_PAIR.md` Sage's bound 0.045162 is not used: the generator is proved by an exact search (`exact_last_pair.py`), from the archimedean bound as stated by Cremona–Prickett–Siksek 2006, Theorem 15, and their Theorem 2 for points in `E⁰`. Are these applied correctly: the model minimal, `24P ∈ E⁰` at every prime, `h(x) − ĥ ≤ 4.444` on `E⁰`, and no point with `x = a/e²`, `|a|, e² ≤ 110`? (f) Section 9 there: the curves of the argument are the twists of an unramified double cover of the curve `u² = F0·F8, v² = GL·GU`, and the class `d` is rational at every rational point of that curve. Is that right, and is the determination of the possible `d` a standard 2-descent on that cover that could replace the Gaussian descent for this purpose? (g) Theorem C there (section 9): the point `(2τ(τ² − 1)(τ² + 1)², 4τ(τ² − 1)²(τ² + 1)²)` has infinite order on `E₁` for every rational `τ ≠ 0, ±1`. The proof is four short steps and ten factored polynomials (`degenerate_section_polys.txt`); the step to check by hand is the specialisation (ii). This is the smallest self-contained new statement in the repository and a good place to start. (h) `height_enclosure.py` uses the bounds of Cremona, Prickett and Siksek (J. Number Theory 116, 2006) between the naive and the canonical height, with the constants 1.946 and 2.14 and their Table 1, as read by an automated reader.  Are they quoted correctly? (i) **Theorem D** (section 11 there) and `scripts/cover_descent/`: for (820,799) and (889,320), which the census counts apart on Magma's two-cover descent (they are also among the 119 ratios excluded in Lean, `LAST_PAIR.md`, section 15; the census is not changed by that), the argument is one page and a local computation that leaves no class. Three things to check, in this order. *First, the paper proof:* Lemma S1 (a number of square norm is a rational number times a square, with a unique squarefree class) and **Lemma S2, the support step**, with its inert, split and ramified cases; S2 uses unique factorisation in `ℤ[i]`. Only the four common-divisor statements of S3 were in Lean when the section was written. *Second, the local test:* is the set of classes that `cover_descent.py` (about 150 lines) allows at a prime never too small? The thresholds it uses are proved in section 14 (R1 to R5); `cover_bruteforce.py` (60 lines, no code in common) reaches the same final sets by exhaustion modulo `p^N`, though not the same set at every single prime (`cover_certificate_*.txt` say where they differ). A third program, written from the prose of the section by a separate reviewer instance (`scripts/cover_descent/third/`; `LAST_PAIR.md`, section 16), adds certified lower bounds: on 3,024 pairs (ratio, prime) the descent rejects no class for which an explicit local point was found, and at 3,006 of them every class that an exhaustion keeps is allowed. *Third, since 24 November 2026, a Lean proof of the same conclusion by a slightly different route* (`MagicSquareOfSquares/CoverDescent.lean`, `CoverDescentTable.lean`; section 15 there): the classes are obtained in integer coordinates, with no unique factorisation, and the local exclusions are finite checks evaluated by the kernel, for 119 ratios, (820,799) and (889,320) among them. For those 119 ratios nothing outside Lean is used; what a reader can check there is that the statements say what is claimed. These ratios are in the census on other grounds (ranks, or Magma for the two named); the Lean result does not change the census. Section 12 has a finer version (`tuple_descent.py`, four classes), where the point to check is the closure of the allowed sets under the class of a unit.

A question for a number theorist of any kind, not only for this audit: are Theorem 1, Theorem 1′, the Lemma and Theorem 3′ of `PAPER.md`, on the two ratios of a magic square of squares, known? (The congruences in Theorems 2 and 3 follow from Rabern's Theorem 1.4 and Woll's Corollary 4.3 once the Lemma is known; `PAPER.md` says so.)

## What already has an automatic check

`check_evidence.py` re-tests every recorded `t`, checks that every recorded generator lies on its curve and that the `E0L` models are the right curves, and compares the table with the JSON output of the scripts. `height_sides.py`, `height_precision.py` (the same heights at 200 bits) and `height_certify.py` are described in questions 3 and 5. `eclib_check.py` and `magma_check.py` are the second and third programs for the rank bounds. None of these proves a height, a rank or a saturation.
