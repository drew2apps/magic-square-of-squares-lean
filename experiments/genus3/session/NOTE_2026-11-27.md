## 1. A symmetry of the whole system: the ratio and the parameter can be exchanged

Each of the four corner norms is unchanged by `(m, n, u, v) -> (u, v, m, n)`, and so is the centre
form `F4 = 2(m² + n²)(u² + v²)` (which satisfies `L + U = 2·F4`, `F0 + F8 = F4/2`).
Check: `verify_swap_and_genus3.py` (sympy, symbolic). On the Gaussian pairs the exchange is
`x + iy -> y + ix = i·conj(x + iy)` for P0, P8, PL and the identity for PU.

I did not find this in the documents I searched (`grep` for the obvious phrasings); it may be known.

Consequences, each elementary:

- A solution has two edge ratios, `m : n` and `u : v`. Closing a ratio closes it in both roles.
- With the census as counted: a solution at one of the nine open ratios has `t = u/v` whose
  reduced ratio is not a closed ratio. The 324 pairs (open ratio, open ratio up to `t -> ±t^±1`)
  were tested: at most one of the five forms is a square. So such a solution has `t` of height
  above 1000 (above the tier-D bounds if those are used).
- `F4` a square forces `2(m² + n²)(u² + v²)` to be a square. At a square-type ratio `u² + v²` is
  twice a square, at a twice-square ratio it is a square, with no hypothesis. Under (R) the two
  ratios of a solution are of the two different degenerate types.
- (R) for the four corner forms is equivalent to its mirror: at every ratio, a point of the corner
  curve has `u² + v²` a square or twice a square. At degenerate ratios this is a new test of (R)
  that the stage-3 point searches can apply to anything they find.

In the coordinates `r = m/n`, `t = u/v` and `c(x) = (x − 1)/(x + 1)` (the partner map) the eight
branch curves are graphs of Möbius maps: `t = ±i·r` (F0), `t = ±i/r` (F8), `c(t) = ±i·c(r)` (L),
`c(t) = ±i/c(r)` (U). These are eight elements of the octahedral group over ℚ(i), closed under
inversion (which is the exchange above); this is where Klein's octahedral forms `f` and `W` of
`LAST_PAIR.md` come from.

## 2. The genus-3 quotient `y² = F0·F8·L·U` splits over ℚ into three elliptic curves

With `x = t²`, `z = x + 1/x`, `A = m²n²`, `B = m⁴ + n⁴`, `C = S²`, `D = 2S² − 16m²n²`, `S = m² + n²`:
`F0·F8 = x(Az + B)`, `L·U = 4x(Cz + D)`, and the Jacobian is isogenous over ℚ to `E1 × E2 × E3`,

    E1: w² = (z² − 4)(Az + B)(Cz + D),   E2: w² = (z + 2)(Az + B)(Cz + D),   E3: w² = (z − 2)(Az + B)(Cz + D).

Check: the trace of Frobenius of the genus-3 curve equals `a_p(E1) + a_p(E2) + a_p(E3)` at 12 primes
for (627,364) (`verify_swap_and_genus3.py`). `FOUR_POINTS.md` mentions this genus-3 curve only as
an obstacle; I did not find its factors used anywhere.

## 3. (627,364): the corner curve over ℚ(i) is not a ℚ-curve, so the analytic route is closed

The report's first suggestion was to reduce the rank over ℚ(i) to two ranks over ℚ. For
`L1·L3·M1·M2` (`kcurve_not_qcurve.py`):

- `j ∉ ℚ` (already in `KCTP_627.md`);
- the conjugate `j` is not among the six `j`-invariants of the 2-power isogeny class;
- the conductor has norm `2⁶·5²·29²·37·181·263²·991²·23490241·395249137273 ≈ 5.7·10³⁹`: the primes
  above 37, 181, 23490241, 395249137273 occur once, so the conductor is not stable under
  conjugation and the curve is not isogenous to its conjugate (nor to a twist of it by a character
  unramified there).

So `L(E/K, s)` belongs to a genuine Bianchi form of level norm about `5.7·10³⁹`: no theorem
"`L ≠ 0` gives rank 0" applies (the report found none for non-base-change curves), and the value
would need about `10²⁰` terms anyway.

Reading of the evidence, not a result: the pairing vanishes on all of Sel² on every curve of the
class and every class lifts to a 4-covering, so rank 0 would need `Ш ⊇ (ℤ/8)²`. Rank 2 is the more
likely answer. If so, this curve can never close (627,364) (elliptic Chabauty needs rank at most 1
over ℚ(i)), and the useful computation is a point search on the 4-coverings to prove rank 2 and
retire the route, not a deeper descent to prove rank 0.

## 4. What the report changes in the plan (my reading)

- No published result proves (R), a special case, or the census. Auel–Singer (arXiv:2609.09351)
  confirm general type, `q = 0`, simply connected: no map to an abelian variety, so the Faltings
  route of roadmap 3c is closed, and any Brauer–Manin argument must use covers ramified along the
  degenerate locus, which is what the four-class descent is.
- Density-one (R): the corner curves themselves are locally soluble for a positive proportion of
  ratios (the fibres over `m = 0`, `m = n` and `m² + n² = 0` each have a geometrically integral
  component over the residue field, checked by hand; the repository's own density 0.2766% says the
  same), so
  Loughran–Smeets applied to the family of curves gives nothing. A density theorem would have to be
  about the twisted family, in the style of Koymans–Pagano–Sofos; that is research, not a
  computation.
