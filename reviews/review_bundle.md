### F4. Cassels–Tate pairing over Q(i) (claim 4) (17:30)
**The Hilbert-reciprocity shortcut (`cassels2`, ctp.gp:282-308).** My reading of the argument: at a finite place P outside PL (PL = bad places + primes of beta, beta' + primes above 3, 5, 7 added by the drivers), with P odd and all of beta, beta', e_i - e_j units, write the conic point Gamma_i = Gamma0/pi^d with Gamma0 primitive integral, d = v_P(Z_i). Because Q_i(Gamma) = Q_i(w) = 0, L_i(w) = 2B(Gamma,w) = 2B(Gamma0,w)/pi^d, and B(Gamma0,w) is a P-unit as soon as the reduction of the local point w is not the reduction of Gamma0 on the (smooth) reduced conic. Then (L_i(w), beta'_i)_P = v_P(Z_i)*[beta'_i non-square] = (Z_i, beta'_i)_P, and Hilbert reciprocity turns the sum over all P outside PL into the sum over PL, which is what `corr` computes. Unit ambiguity of the generator Z_i drops out by reciprocity; homogeneous rescaling of w drops out because beta'_1 beta'_2 beta'_3 is a square; dengen/ggcd do compute a generator of the lcm of the denominator ideals. **I find the argument correct**, with one hypothesis it should state: a local point whose reductions avoid the three points Gamma0_i mod P must exist; at most 6 points of the reduced genus-1 covering are excluded, and #D(F_q) >= q+1-2sqrt(q) > 6 needs q >= 13. Over Q(i) every residue field outside PL (with 3, 5, 7 added) has q >= 13, so the shortcut is complete there; over Q, p = 11 is not covered unless it is in PL (the drivers add only 3, 5, 7).
