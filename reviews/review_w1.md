## Statements in plain maths

- **`E1K`**: E1 : y² = x³ + 8d·x² − 8d²·x with d = 2+√2, over K = ℚ(√2) ⊂ ℝ (LemmaDBridge.lean:40; the diff does not
  change it). Δ = 98304·d⁶ ≠ 0. The model is genuine and the field is the real quadratic field. Neither is degenerate.
- **`E1K_finrank_eq_zero`**: the ℤ-rank of E1(K) is 0. On its own, `finrank = 0` would also hold for a module that is
  not finitely generated and has infinite rank. Here it means what it says, because E1(K) is finitely generated by
  `E1K_fg` (Stoll's Mordell–Weil theorem, used through class-group finiteness and units being FG). The finite
  generation is passed explicitly at E1RankGlue.lean:405.
- **`E1K_isOfFinAddOrder`**: every P ∈ E1(K) has finite order. **`E1K_finite`**: E1(K) is finite. The group is
  nontrivial because it contains T and (4, 8√2).
- **`lemmaD_of_torsion`**: if every nonsingular affine point of finite order has x ∈ {0, ±4, ±(12+8√2)}, then
  LemmaD holds. LemmaD says: every rational point of w² = 2(x²+1)(x⁴−2x³+2x²+2x+1) has x = ±1. The hypothesis is
  exactly the remaining (C3–C4) and is not proved. It is true mathematically, since the ten points are Z/10, so the
  theorem is not vacuous.
- **`DescentW a b`**: for every (x, y) with y² = x(x²+ax+b) and x ≠ 0, either x is a square or b = x·u² for some u.
  **`DescentW' a b`**: the same on Y² = X(X² − 2aX + a² − 4b), with the alternative a² − 4b = X·v². Here
  b = −16ε² ≡ −1 and a² − 4b = 192ε² ≡ 3 (with d = √2·ε). So the two statements say exactly im α ⊆ {1, −1} and
  im α′ ⊆ {1, 3}.
- **Descent lemmas** (E1Descent.lean). Every hypothesis is satisfiable and is used at a real instance in
  `descentE1` and `descentE1'`:
  - `not_sq_odd`: p prime, r ≠ 0, p ∤ U ⇒ n² ≠ r²·p·U.
  - `not_sq_oddim`: im V odd, r ≠ 0 ⇒ n² ≠ r²V.
  - `not_sq_neg`: σ : ℤ[√2] → ℝ an injective ring map, r ≠ 0, σ(F) < 0 ⇒ n² ≠ r²F.
  - `quartic_of_point`: x ≠ 0 on the curve and x = ι(c)w² ⇒ there are n, r, s, t with r, s, t ≠ 0, not both s, t
    divisible by p, and n² = r²·c(c²s⁴ + Acs²t² + Bt⁴).
  - `exists_sqfree`: x ≠ 0 ⇒ x = ι(δ)w² with δ squarefree.
  - `prime_dvd_B`: δ squarefree, p | δ, p ∤ B ⇒ no such (n, r, s, t).
  - `classify1` / `classify2`: δ = unit·√2^i·3^j with i, j ≤ 1.
  - `V1` / `V2`: δ = √2·w with √2 ∤ w is excluded (odd valuation).
  - `M1`: δ = 4u with u² = ε², im u odd and √2 ∤ u is excluded (mod 2 and valuation).
  - `real_excl`: σ(δ) < 0 and σ(768+512√2) > 0 ⇒ excluded on E1′.
- **Glue.** `cover` shows that every P is 2R or 2R + T, using Mathlib's group law. It needs only ψ∘φ = [2]
  (checked against Mathlib's doubling formula), ψ(Q+T′) = ψ(Q), explicit preimages, and x(P+T)·x(P) = b. No
  homomorphism property of φ or ψ is assumed. Then `finrank_eq_zero_of_cover` gives [G:2G] ≤ 2. Stoll's formula
  [G:2G] = 2^r·#G[2] together with #G[2] ≥ 2 (T ≠ 0) gives r = 0. This is correct.

## The descent, re-derived by hand

- **Which primes can divide δ.** Only primes dividing B: on E1, B = −16ε² = −√2⁸ε², so only √2. On E1′,
  B′ = 3·√2¹²·ε², so √2 and 3. The constant d = √2·ε adds no new prime. 3 is inert because 2 is a non-residue
  mod 3; this is `three_prime`. The units are ±ε^k, so modulo squares they are {±1, ±ε}. This needs ε² = 3+2√2 to be
  the fundamental Pell solution, and its minimality is proved at Zsqrt2.lean:210. So E1 has 8 classes and E1′ has 16.
- **E1.** No exclusion is possible at a real place: x²+Ax+B has two real roots of opposite sign at both places. That
  makes the 2-adic test necessary, and it is used correctly.
  - ±√2 and ±ε√2 are excluded by `V1`. I re-derived the √2-valuations 3, 7 and 9 for its three sub-cases.
  - ±ε are excluded by `M1`, after writing x = 16ε·(w/2)². In the three sub-cases, V ≡ ±uε² or uε² modulo π²,
    which has odd imaginary part, or else the valuation is 3. Re-derived.
- **E1′.** A′² − 4B′ = 16B < 0 at both real places, so the quadratic factor is positive there. The sign of F is
  therefore the sign of σ(δ), and `real_excl` kills every class that is negative at some place: −1, ±ε, √2, and the
  negatives. The two totally positive classes containing √2, namely √2ε and 3√2ε, go by `V2` (valuations 3, 7, 11,
  13, re-derived). All 16 cases at E1Descent.lean:549–582 are covered.
- **iota2 and s2.** `iota2` (√2 ↦ s2) is the intended embedding. It is injective, and `iota2_A1/B1/A2/B2` pin the
  constants. `s2_mul_s2` proves s2² = 2. The `decide` calls are on closed ℤ[√2] arithmetic, where a false
  proposition cannot be accepted.
